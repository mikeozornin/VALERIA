# V.A.L.E.R.I.A. License FAQ

This FAQ explains common scenarios in plain language. It is not legal advice and
does not replace `LICENSE.md` or a signed Commercial or Enterprise agreement. If
there is a conflict, the applicable legal agreement controls.

## Is V.A.L.E.R.I.A. open source?

No. The Community Edition is source-available under the custom V.A.L.E.R.I.A.
Community License 1.0. The source can be inspected, used, and modified within
that license, but restrictions on SaaS, commercial redistribution, OEM use, and
white-label use mean it should not be described as OSI-approved open source.

## Can companies use V.A.L.E.R.I.A. for free?

Yes. Organizations of any size may use Community Components internally without
a paid V.A.L.E.R.I.A. license. Company size, revenue, and employee count are not
license triggers.

## Do I need a license for internal company use?

The Community License is the license for internal use; no separate paid license
is required if the use stays within its terms. This includes internal
development, automation, agent orchestration, testing, CI/CD, project work, and
infrastructure management.

## Are there limits on internal AI agents or usage?

No artificial Community limits are imposed on agents, managers, tasks,
pipelines, internal users, projects, repositories, execution environments,
internal servers, or local models.

## Can I use V.A.L.E.R.I.A. to generate commercial code?

Yes. You may sell independently created code and other work product produced
with assistance from V.A.L.E.R.I.A. Revenue from output does not by itself
require a Commercial License. You remain responsible for reviewing outputs and
for any third-party model or content terms.

## Can I use it for paid freelance or consulting projects?

Yes, when You use it as Your tool and the client receives code, reports, advice,
or another independent result rather than access to the platform itself.

## Can I self-host it?

Yes. You may operate multiple internal instances on private infrastructure,
servers, containers, or virtual machines and connect local or remote models,
subject to the Community License and third-party terms.

## Can I modify the source code?

Yes. You may inspect, privately modify, and extend Community Components for
permitted uses. Distribution of modified versions is more limited and must
follow Section 5 of `LICENSE.md`. Third-party directories retain their own
licenses.

## Can I create plugins, integrations, agents, and AI managers?

Yes, for Community-permitted uses. Whether a separately distributed plugin is a
derivative work depends on its technical and legal relationship to
V.A.L.E.R.I.A.; the license does not predetermine that question.

## Can I use my own or a self-hosted LLM?

Yes. The Community License does not restrict model choice. You must comply with
the model, software, dataset, API, and infrastructure terms that apply to Your
chosen stack.

## Can a contractor operate an internal deployment for my company?

Yes, if the contractor acts solely on Your behalf and under Your control for
Your Internal Use. You remain responsible for compliance. The contractor may not
reuse the deployment to sell platform access to unrelated customers.

## Can affiliates share an internal deployment?

Controlled Affiliates may share it for the controlled group's Internal Use if
each entity independently complies with the Community License. Unrelated
companies, customers, and joint ventures may require different treatment.

## Can I install V.A.L.E.R.I.A. for a client and charge for the work?

Yes, if it is a dedicated Client-Controlled Deployment and all conditions in
Section 5 of `LICENSE.md` are met. The client must receive the licensed source,
accept the Community License, control the environment and access, receive
required notices, and independently comply. You may charge for genuine
installation, configuration, migration, training, maintenance, or consulting.

## Can I continue managing the client's installation?

You may administer it as the client's contractor if the client controls the
deployment and You act solely on the client's behalf. A Commercial License is
required if You retain service-provider control and sell access, pool customers,
or turn it into provider-operated hosted service. Ambiguous responsibility and
cloud-account arrangements should be reviewed before deployment.

## Can I provide customers with a V.A.L.E.R.I.A. dashboard?

Not under the Community License when customers can access, configure, control,
or invoke material platform functionality. A Commercial License is required,
including for a dedicated customer-facing instance operated by You.

## Does an API avoid the hosted-service restriction?

No. External machine-to-machine access is treated like human access when it
exposes material V.A.L.E.R.I.A. functionality. An API that only delivers a
finished independent result can be different; design and control matter.

## Can I build a SaaS using V.A.L.E.R.I.A.?

You may use it internally to build and operate Your own unrelated SaaS product.
You need a Commercial License if Your customers receive V.A.L.E.R.I.A. agent,
pipeline, management, API, or equivalent platform functionality as the service.

## Can I resell a modified version?

Not under the Community License. Selling or commercially redistributing a
modified version as software requires express Commercial rights. OEM, source,
binary, recipient-modification, and sublicensing rights must be written in the
Order Form.

## Can I remove V.A.L.E.R.I.A. branding?

Private internal interface changes may be modifications permitted for Internal
Use. Commercial white-label use requires an express grant. Product branding is
different from legal notices: required copyright and third-party notices may
not be removed even when white-label rights are granted.

## Does hosting for free make external access permitted?

Not necessarily. The Community restriction concerns making material platform
functionality available as a Hosted Service, not only charging a fee. A public
demo, educational deployment, or non-commercial distribution may need a
fact-specific review under the exact license text.

## What is included in Enterprise licensing?

Only components expressly identified as Enterprise Components and listed in an
accepted Enterprise Order Form. They may include SSO/SAML, advanced RBAC,
central audit, policy and compliance tools, multi-instance administration,
premium integrations, support, or an SLA. Community rights remain independent.

## Do I get Enterprise features because I can see source elsewhere?

No. Enterprise Components are governed by their own agreement. Conversely,
using Community Components inside a large organization does not turn them into
Enterprise Components.

## What happens if my paid agreement ends?

Paid hosted, OEM, white-label, distribution, support, and Enterprise rights end
as the applicable agreement provides. Independent Community rights remain
available if You can and do comply fully with the Community License.

## Who owns code and reports created through V.A.L.E.R.I.A.?

The license does not claim Your independent inputs or work product. It also does
not guarantee that AI-generated material is copyrightable, non-infringing, or
owned by You. Applicable law and third-party provider terms can affect the
answer.

## Does the Community License cover everything in the repository?

No. Third-party materials retain their own terms. `NOTICE.md` identifies known
included components, including an AGPL-licensed directory and an MIT-licensed
vendor notice. Package-managed dependencies also require compliance with their
upstream licenses.
